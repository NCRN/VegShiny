Data Trends Over Time

These graphs show how estimates of forest vegetation abundance, size, and plot occupancy have changed across monitoring cycles for a selected park. Each line represents a species (or all species combined), and each point on the line represents the mean value for that species during a given monitoring cycle. Confidence intervals are represented by opaque ribbons of the same color and are optionally shown on the figure. The table shows the same data in a structured format. You can view this by selecting the "Data table" tab at the top of the page.

There are three options for selecting species to graph:

  • Most common species: This displays the species with the highest mean abundance, basal area, or proportion of plot occupied. You can adjust the number of species to display.
  • Pick individual species: This allows you to pick which species to display. Click in the box to see a list of species or type to search. Click on a species or hit "Enter" to choose it. To unselect a species, click on the X next to its name or press the backspace key. You can choose as many species as you like.
  • All species combined: This graphs the summed data for every observed species matching the chosen data measures.

Depending on the type of plant selected there are a variety of measurements that can be graphed (NCRN forest vegetation monitoring protocol):

  • Abundance: This is an estimate of how many individuals of a given species are found in each forested hectare of a park. A hectare is 2.47 acres, or approximately the size of two football fields. This measure is available for trees, saplings, tree seedlings, shrubs, shrub seedlings, and vines on trees. It is expressed as #/ha.
  • Basal Area: This is the area of the cross-section of a tree or sapling at 1.37 meters (4.5 feet) above the ground. This measure is available for trees and saplings.
  • Percent of Plots Occupied: This measure indicates the percentage of monitoring plots in a park that contain a particular species. This measure, along with the map, can indicate how widespread a species and is available for all plant types.
  • Percent Cover: This is a measure of what percent of a plot is covered by the leaves and stems of a plant species. It can be used as a measure of size or abundance. This is particularly useful for species which grow as numerous small individuals, such as blades of grass, which cannot be easily counted. This measure is available for understory plants.

Gaps in a species line indicate that the species was not observed during that monitoring cycle. Cycles with no observations for any species of the selected plant type are excluded from the figure and table entirely.

Statistical Details

Statistical calculations were performed using the NPSForVeg R package developed by Schmit et al. (2024). Mean values (averages) are always calculated by first summing the values for the monitoring plots in a park and then dividing by the number of plots in the park. The means and confidence intervals are first calculated on a per-plot basis and then converted to a per-hectare basis. The 95% confidence intervals are more complicated to calculate and depend on the type of data graphed.

Abundance: The 95% confidence interval is calculated by fitting the number of plants found in each plot to a negative binomial distribution. Using negative binomial estimation ensures that the 95% confidence interval will never include negative numbers of plants, and often results in confidence intervals that are not symmetric around the mean.

Basal Area: Basal area measurements often do not fit any standard statistical distribution. To account for this, the 95% confidence interval is calculated by bootstrapping the observed data.

Percent of Plots Occupied: 95% confidence intervals are calculated by fitting the data to a binomial distribution.

Percent Cover: Like basal area, confidence intervals are calculated using bootstrapping.

When only one monitoring plot exists for a park during a given cycle, confidence intervals cannot be estimated and are shown as NA in the table and omitted from the figure.

Data by Park and Species

These graphs allow you to see estimates of the abundance, size, and percent of monitoring plots occupied by particular species in each park. The graphs show the mean and the 95% confidence intervals (bars) for the selected species. The table shows Data to Graph (abundance, basal area, and proportion of plots occupied) at a park, in a given time period. You can view this by selecting the "Data table" tab at the top of the page.

There are four options for selecting species to graph:

  • Most common species: This displays the species with the highest mean abundance, basal area, or proportion of plot occupied. You can adjust the number of species to display.
  • Least common species: This displays the species with the lowest mean abundance, basal area, or proportion of plot occupied. You can adjust the number of species to display.
  • Pick individual species: This allows you to pick which species to display. Click in the box to see a list of species or type to search. Click on a species or hit "Enter" to choose it. To unselect a species, click on the X next to its name or press the backspace key. You can choose as many species as you like.
  • All species combined: This graphs the combined data for every observed species matching the chosen data measures.

Depending on the type of plant selected there are a variety of measurements that can be graphed (NCRN forest vegetation monitoring protocol):

  • Abundance: This is an estimate of how many individuals of a given species are found in each forested hectare of a park. A hectare is 2.47 acres, or approximately the size of two football fields. This measure is available for trees, saplings, tree seedlings, shrubs, shrub seedlings, and vines on trees. It is expressed as #/ha.
  • Basal Area: This is the area of the cross-section of a tree or sapling at 1.37 meters (4.5 feet) above the ground. This measure is available for trees and saplings. It is expressed as meters2/ha.
  • Percent of Plots Occupied: This measure indicates the percentage of monitoring plots in a park that contain a particular species. This measure, along with the map, can indicate how widespread a species and is available for all plant types.
  • Percent Cover: This is a measure of what percent of a plot is covered by the leaves and stems of a plant species. It can be used as a measure of size or abundance. This is particularly useful for species which grow as numerous small individuals, such as blades of grass, which cannot be easily counted. This measure is available for understory plants.

Once you have selected the primary data you wish to graph, you can add comparison data. Three options are available:

  • Park: This will allow you to select a second comparison park. If you have chosen to graph the "Most common species", then the most common species from the first park selected will be displayed. If you have chosen "Pick individual species", then you can only pick species present in the first park.
  • Growth Stage: This allows you to compare two different growth stages of the same species. You can make comparisons between trees, saplings, and tree seedlings, or between shrubs and shrub seedlings. This option is not available for understory plants or vines on trees, as only one growth stage of these species is monitored.
  • Time: This option allows you to make comparisons between different sets of years.

Clicking on the red reset button at the top of the data input panel will clear all applied filters.

You also have the ability to change a variety of settings to customize the figure in the Display Options popup, including:

  • Base Data Color: This adjusts the bar color of the species generated in the "Data:" panel.
  • Comparison Data Color: This adjusts the bar color of the species generated in the "Comparison Data:" panel. This feature is not available when no comparison data is selected.
  • Error Bar Thickness: This slider adjusts the thickness of the error bars.
  • Font Size: This slider adjusts the font size of the axes, legend, and title. This feature is disabled on smaller and mobile screens.

Clicking on the red reset button at the top of the "Display Options" panel will reset all display settings.

Statistical Details

Statistical calculations were performed using the NPSForVeg R package developed by Schmit et al. (2024). Mean values (averages) are always calculated by first summing the values for the monitoring plots in a park and then dividing by the number of plots in the park. The means and confidence intervals are first calculated on a per-plot basis and then converted to a per-hectare basis. The 95% confidence intervals are more complicated to calculate and depend on the type of data graphed.

Abundance: The 95% confidence interval is calculated by bootstrapping the observed data.

Basal Area: Basal area measurements often do not fit any standard statistical distribution. To account for this, the 95% confidence interval is calculated by bootstrapping the observed data.

Percent of Plots Occupied: 95% confidence intervals are calculated by fitting the data to a binomial distribution.

Percent Cover: Confidence intervals are calculated using bootstrapping.
